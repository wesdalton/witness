# Decisions to make after the fixed allocation screen

Prepared before interpreting its full treatment contrast. A promising story is
not enough to justify a larger run. The next paid batch must resolve a concrete
uncertainty and pass the existing validity/budget gates.

1. **Allocation replication:** useful if the completed screen identifies a
   potentially substantial, interpretable allocation response. Freeze fresh seeds
   and precision goals; do not call the adaptive screen confirmatory. A broad
   confidence interval around a small estimate is inconclusive, not equivalence.
2. **Evidence quality after access:** the more distinctive candidate mechanism.
   Hold initial access and evidence fixed, randomize peer context, and measure
   generated programs on unseen counterfactual cases. Do not condition on targets
   selected after treatment in the allocation study.
3. **Belief versus artifact mismatch:** a correct investigation can coexist with
   an incorrect verbal judgment or reversed probability. First clarify probability
   direction and provide an exact finite claim. A replicated belief effect alone
   is close to existing social-influence research; the joint artifact result must
   add something substantive before making a novelty claim.

The new inventory query harness prepares option 2 and improves option 3's
measurement. Its independent oracles and replay gates are free infrastructure
checks, not demonstrations that the task is difficult for a model. A small no-peer
capability calibration should precede any comparison. If every program succeeds
in one straightforward query, do not spend on a large peer sweep solely because
the task has many rows or sounds complex.

Possible further development includes auditing an inherited near-correct program,
or a harder finite task with combinatorial dependencies and two independently
checked reference implementations. These are hypotheses for task development,
not permission to select failures or tune wording to manufacture a harmful effect.
If actual live peer reports are studied, preserve all producer attempts and report
their selection policy. Four authored statements remain authored statements.

Provider-side strict tool schemas can prevent avoidable structural errors in a
new configuration. This does not solve budget-compliance or reasoning errors.
